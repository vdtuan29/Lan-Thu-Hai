🌌 Story 2: Stephen Hawking – A Great Mind Soaring Free in an Immobile Body If anyone ever proved that the human spirit is stronger than any physical law on earth, it was Stephen Hawking. Diagnosed with a fatal disease and given only two years to live in his twenties, he defied his destiny to live for more than half a century, becoming the greatest theoretical physicist since Albert Einstein. A Death Sentence at Age 21 Stephen Hawking was born in 1942 in England. As a child, he was a bright but not overly remarkable student, even somewhat lazy. However, his peers and teachers recognized his unique thinking and affectionately nicknamed him "Einstein." At age 21, while a passionate graduate student at the University of Cambridge, Hawking began to notice strange symptoms: he frequently tripped, struggled to tie his shoes, and his speech became slurred. After a series of tests, doctors delivered a brutal diagnosis: he had ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis). This disease destroys the motor neurons, causing muscles to waste away until the body is completely paralyzed. The doctor bluntly stated he had a maximum of two years to live. It was a death sentence for a young man. Hawking fell into deep depression. He locked himself in his room, listened to Wagner's music, and watched his future slam shut. But during those dark days, a realization hit him: "When one's expectations are reduced to zero, one really appreciates everything one does have." He decided not to give up. He wanted to finish his PhD before he died. A Cosmic Voyage from a Wheelchair The cruel disease ravaged Hawking's body exactly as predicted, but it was powerless against his genius mind. He eventually lost the ability to walk and became permanently confined to a wheelchair. By 1985, following an emergency tracheotomy, he lost his voice entirely. The world thought Stephen Hawking was completely silenced and isolated. However, thanks to technological advances, he was equipped with a specialized computer system attached to his wheelchair. He could only move a single cheek muscle to select letters on a screen, allowing a voice synthesizer to speak for him. Typing at a speed of just a few words per minute, Hawking wrote "A Brief History of Time"—the best-selling popular science book of all time, explaining the complex mysteries of the universe, black holes, and the Big Bang in the simplest language. Though his body was imprisoned in a wheelchair, Hawking's mind soared freely through the most distant galaxies. He challenged the laws of black holes, proving that they are not completely "black" but instead emit radiation (now known as Hawking Radiation). He lived an incredibly vibrant life: traveling the world, experiencing zero gravity on a specialized aircraft, appearing in television shows, and never losing his sharp, sarcastic sense of humor. Stephen Hawking passed away in 2018 at the age of 76, meaning he survived for 55 years after his initial death sentence. He proved to humanity that: "However difficult life may seem, there is always something you can do and succeed at. It matters that you don't just give up."
