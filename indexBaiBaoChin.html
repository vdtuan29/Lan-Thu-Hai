The Bitten Apple and the Philosophy That Changed the World from a Garage

Today, the iPhone, the iPad, and the Mac computer have become familiar devices that have completely transformed how humanity lives, communicates, and works. Behind these elegant, iconic, and sleek products lies the life of an eccentric, hot-tempered man who possessed a vision decades ahead of his time. He was the one who turned a company on the brink of bankruptcy into the most valuable tech empire on the planet. That man was Steve Jobs.

The Abandoned Child and the Lesson of Perfection from His Adoptive Father


Steve Jobs was born in 1955 in San Francisco, USA. Right at birth, he was given up by his biological parents and adopted by a working-class couple, Paul and Clara Jobs.

Steve's adoptive father, Paul Jobs, was a skilled machinist. He was the one who passed down to Steve a passion for electronic components and meticulous design lines. One particular lesson from his father stayed with Steve for the rest of his life: while the two were building a wooden fence in their backyard, Paul meticulously smoothed and painted even the back of the fence—the side facing the wall that no one would ever see. A young Steve asked his father why he wasted so much effort on it, and Paul replied: "But we know it's there. For a true carpenter, even the back of a cabinet facing the wall must be made with the finest wood." This philosophy of absolute perfection later became the soul of every Apple product.

Steve Jobs was a brilliant but rebellious student who always found school boring. He went to college but decided to drop out after just six months because he did not want to waste his adoptive parents' life savings on classes he didn't care about. However, he stayed on campus for another 18 months to drop in on art classes, including a calligraphy class—a seemingly useless subject that eventually became the reason why the Mac computer possessed the world's first beautiful typography.

The Crushing Blow: Being Fired from the Company He Co-Founded


In 1976, inside his family's cramped garage, a 21-year-old Steve Jobs and his tech-genius friend Steve Wozniak hand-assembled the very first computers and founded Apple. Guided by Jobs' innate marketing talent and unique aesthetic vision, Apple grew at a lightning pace, becoming a hundred-million-dollar company in just a few years.

However, Jobs' arrogance, demanding nature, and extreme personality led to fierce clashes with the Board of Directors. In 1895, at the age of 30, Steve Jobs suffered the biggest shock of his life: he was officially fired from Apple—the very company he had given his soul to build.

For a rising star standing at the peak of success, this was a humiliating and deeply public failure. Jobs was devastated for months and even thought about fleeing Silicon Valley. But the creative fire inside him never burned out. He later reflected:"Getting fired from Apple was the best thing that could have ever happened to me. The heaviness of being successful was replaced by the lightness of being a beginner again, less sure about everything. It freed me to enter one of the most creative periods of my life."

During his years away from Apple, Jobs founded the computer company NeXT and acquired Pixar Animation Studios—the studio that later produced Toy Story, reshaping the history of global cinema.

The Return of the King and the Revolution That Transformed Humanity


By 1996, Apple had fallen into a severe crisis and was only months away from total bankruptcy. In an act of desperation, Apple acquired NeXT and brought Steve Jobs back, first as an advisor and later as the Chief Executive Officer (CEO).

The return of Steve Jobs stands as one of the most miraculous chapters in global business history. He ruthlessly cut 70% of Apple's redundant projects, focusing all energy on combining cutting-edge technology with minimalist art. He launched the colorful iMac, the iPod digital music player that revolutionized the music industry, and the ultimate pinnacle arrived in 2007—the introduction of the iPhone.

The iPhone was not just a phone; it was a revolution that redefined the mobile information age, completely erasing traditional physical keyboards to make way for a fluid multi-touch screen. Steve Jobs single-handedly raised Apple from the ashes into a monumental empire.

Even when facing a brutal battle with pancreatic cancer, Jobs worked exhaustively until his final days. In his iconic 2005 Stanford University commencement speech, he left a timeless message for the younger generation: "Stay hungry, stay foolish." He passed away in 2011, leaving behind a massive legacy that fundamentally shaped the face of the modern world.

✨ Deep Lessons from Steve Jobs' Life


• Connecting the dots in your past: You cannot connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backward. The seemingly random calligraphy class or the crushing blow of being fired turned out to be the perfect pieces that created his greatest successes later on.

• Love what you do: Throughout all the ups and downs, the bankruptcies, and the rejection, the only thing that kept Jobs going was his pure love for the work. When you find your true passion, no adversity can break you.

• Your time is limited: Aware of the brevity of life, especially after discovering his illness, Jobs lived and created with maximum urgency. Do not waste your time living someone else's life; have the courage to follow your own heart and intuition.
